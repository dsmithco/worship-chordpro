## Public domain hymns

In the United States, a work published in 1930 or earlier is in the public domain in 2026. Those hymns carry the full chart with words and simple chords for guitar and keyboard. The chord settings on public domain hymns are free to use. Other countries count copyright years differently, so a hymn free in the United States may still be protected where you live.

## Copyrighted songs and CCLI

Songs written after 1930 are protected by copyright. Two examples are 10,000 Reasons and Way Maker. This site lists the title, writers, year, and CCLI song number for each song, and links to SongSelect. A CCLI Church Copyright License lets a church that holds one project, print, and copy lyrics from the CCLI catalog for its own worship services. It does not let a website publish those songs for the public. Streaming a service online needs a separate license.

The build that publishes this site checks every file. It refuses to publish lyrics or chords for a licensed song, and it refuses any public domain claim dated after 1930.

Songs are filed by the first letter of the title, with one folder per song and one file for each version of it. Public domain hymns include the full chart, while copyrighted songs list the title, writers, and year only.
